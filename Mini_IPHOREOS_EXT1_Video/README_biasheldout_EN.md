Duration: 2 minutes 50 seconds. Resolution: 1280 x 720 at 12 frames per second. English on-screen explanations; no voice-over or audio. Companion to [EXT1_Explanation_EN.mp4](EXT1_Explanation_EN.mp4) (Milestone 1) and [EXT1_Memory_Explanation_EN.mp4](EXT1_Memory_Explanation_EN.mp4) (Milestone 2); this one covers the two later milestones that close out the original six-item improvement plan.

**Revision note.** This version was rewritten for viewers who have not read the surrounding project documents, such as an investor or an outside reviewer seeing it for the first time. Internal code names (policy names like `ext1_bias_ref`, file paths, variable names) now appear only as small secondary labels next to a plain-English description, never as the primary on-screen phrase, and no caption uses an em-dash or a double-hyphen. ## What the video shows

- 00:00-00:15: the problem. A robot whose sensor reads a constant distance short and a robot that is simply slower look identical from decisions alone, and why that cost real accuracy in an earlier test (success fell to 86 percent under a steady bias, and to 53 percent under a random one).
- 00:15-00:32: the fix. A schematic reference check: five readings taken once at a known 50cm distance before the real approach starts, so the average of those readings minus 50cm gives a correction that is applied to every later reading.
- 00:32-01:02: a constant 5cm sensor bias, with and without the fix, side by side. Both cars follow the same physical motion; the uncorrected belief reading visibly sits about 5cm high the whole trial, while the corrected one tracks the true distance closely.
- 01:02-01:37: the harder case, a bias that is different and unpredictable in every trial (somewhere between -8 and +8 cm). Same pattern, larger effect: without the fix this is close to a coin flip.
- 01:37-01:50: the freezing discipline behind Milestone 4. Every internal setting was written down and locked in place before testing on a completely fresh batch of randomly generated cases that nobody had seen during three milestones of development.
- 01:50-02:20: a new test: a sensor reading that shows up and looks plausible but is badly wrong (different from a dropout, where no reading arrives at all). One car shows two real glitches from the trace being caught by the robot's own sanity check; the captions then report the honest 100-trial picture, including the one place the bias-corrected version does slightly worse than the rest (a higher abort rate under an unlucky first bad reading).
- 02:20-02:50: the replication summary, side by side on the original test cases and the fresh unseen batch, and what is still entirely untested (a real, physical robot).

The robot's on-screen position is driven directly by the simulator's true distance and elapsed time. The belief readout (red, teal, or violet halo) reports the robot's distance estimate against the true distance, which is where the bias story actually lives; a position-only marker would look almost identical across panels, since distance is sensor-corrected every step regardless of which version is running.

## Evidence and interpretation

Every trace is read directly from `results_ext1/trace_bias_*.json`, `trace_bias_random_*.json` and `trace_outlier_*.json` (seed 10000, generated by `experiments/run_simulation.py`), not re-simulated or adjusted for the video. The two glitch moments shown on screen (at 7.19s and 8.07s of simulated time) are the real `unexpected_outcome` events already present in the trace, not added for effect; the corrected version happens to hit an unlucky corrupted *first* reading in that same scenario (there is no prior belief yet to check it against), which is exactly the honest finding reported in the closing captions, not hidden because it is unflattering.

Every aggregate number spoken in captions is read from the actual evaluation outputs, not from the single illustrative trial on screen: the bias and random-bias success rates (86% and 53%, recovering to 99%) and the bias estimate error (0.42cm) come from `results_ext1/bias_reference_summary.csv`; the outlier success and abort rates (97-98% vs 95%, abort 2% vs 5%) come from `results_ext1/outlier_summary.csv`; the original-vs-fresh-batch replication figures in the final chapter come from `experiments/compare_heldout.py` comparing `results_ext1/` against `results_ext1_heldout/` (seeds 50000-50099). Full derivations and tables are in `Tutorial_EXT1_EN.pdf` (Sections 8-9) and `Mathematical_Model_EXT1.pdf` (the bias-reference and held-out-evaluation sections).

No physical robot data or hardware validation is presented. The Extended system is a desktop cognitive-architecture prototype, not a complete active-inference implementation; see `Tutorial_EXT1_EN.pdf` Section 10 and `Mini_IPHOREOS_Beamer.pdf` for the physical-trial plan and shopping list.

## Files and reproducibility

`manifest_biasheldout_EN.json` records the source file hashes (`cognitive/model.py`, `cognitive/controller.py`, `experiments/run_simulation.py`, `docs/FROZEN_PARAMETERS.md`) and which trace file backs each panel. `preview_biasheldout_EN_5s.png`, `_20s.png`, `_40s.png`, `_65s.png`, `_95s.png`, `_125s.png` and `_155s.png` are still previews, one per chapter.

To rebuild, from the `Mini_IPHOREOS_EXT1` directory: `python tools/make_ext1_bias_heldout_video.py`. Requires Pillow and imageio-ffmpeg (see `requirements.txt`) and a populated `results_ext1/` (run `experiments/run_simulation.py` first if missing); the held-out numbers quoted in captions additionally need `results_ext1_heldout/` (`python experiments/run_simulation.py --trials 100 --seed-start 50000 --out-dir results_ext1_heldout`).
