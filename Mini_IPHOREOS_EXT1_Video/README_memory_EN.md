# Extended-system context-memory video (Milestone 2)

**Video:** [EXT1_Memory_Explanation_EN.mp4](EXT1_Memory_Explanation_EN.mp4)

Duration: 2 minutes 30 seconds. Resolution: 1280 x 720 at 12 frames per second. English on-screen explanations; no voice-over or audio. Companion to [EXT1_Explanation_EN.mp4](EXT1_Explanation_EN.mp4) (Milestone 1); this one covers a different, later finding.

## What the video shows

- 00:00-00:13: the hypothesis -- recognising a previously-seen regime should let the controller adapt faster than estimating it from scratch, and why testing this needed a new scenario (`repeated`: normal/slow alternating every 8 moves).
- 00:13-00:50: attempt 1, a negative result. Two robots (`ext1` vs `ext1_memory`) physically move along their own track in real time, at the actual simulated speed -- visibly crawling in slow segments, moving briskly in normal ones. Neither ever shows a flash; the belief readout stays red (mismatched) for several seconds after every switch, on both sides equally.
- 00:50-01:20: the diagnosis. Watching `ext1` alone: the belief readout takes about the same few seconds to turn teal (matched) again on every single visit, pointing at the context-recognition delay, not the speed estimate itself, as the bottleneck.
- 01:20-02:05: attempt 2, the fix. `ext1` vs `ext1_memory_fast`, same track, same trial -- watch for the violet ring flash around the car and "MEMORY RECALLED" on the right-hand robot on its second and third visits to a regime: the belief readout snaps to match almost immediately, instead of drifting back over several seconds.
- 02:05-02:30: status -- what this milestone established (four of six improvement-plan items now addressed) and what remains open.

The robot's on-screen position is driven directly by the simulator's true distance and elapsed time, so its physical speed on screen is accurate, not stylised -- this is why it visibly crawls during slow segments. The belief readout (red/teal/violet) reports something the position can't show: distance is corrected by direct sensor readings every step regardless of architecture, so a true-vs-believed position marker would barely differ between panels. The memory effect lives specifically in the speed belief, which is what the colour and the flash report. All three panels across the whole video replay the exact same trial (seed 10000), so any difference between them is the architecture, not chance.

## Evidence and interpretation

Every trace is read directly from `results_ext1/trace_repeated_*.json` (seed 10000, generated by `experiments/run_simulation.py`), not re-simulated or adjusted for the video. The violet flashes in the fourth chapter are real: the underlying trace has discontinuities in estimated speed of 1.5-3.4 cm/s at moves 21, 28, 36, 42 and 51 (simulator time 9.7s, 12.8s, 16.4s, 19.1s, 23.0s), consistent with the discrete reuse mechanism firing -- confirmed directly against `cognitive/model.py`'s `_jump_to`, not inferred from the video alone. `ext1` and `ext1_memory` never flash, by construction: jump detection is only ever run on the `ext1_memory_fast` trace, since the other two have no such mechanism at all, even though their own belief can swing by a similar raw amount from ordinary gradual re-weighting.

The reconvergence numbers quoted in the closing chapter (4.3-4.4 decisions on the first visit; 4.7-4.9 vs 3.2-3.3 on later visits) are the 100-trial averages from `results_ext1/reconvergence_summary.csv`, not read off this one illustrative trial -- a single trial is shown for legibility, but the claim rests on the full evaluation, reported in full in `Tutorial_EXT1_EN.pdf` (Section 7) and derived in `Mathematical_Model_EXT1.pdf` (Sections 11-12).

The first design shown (the persistent library, chapter 2) is reported honestly as a negative result, not edited out. The second design (chapter 4) is not claimed to be the only possible fix, or guaranteed to generalise beyond this architecture -- it is the one change that followed directly from the diagnosis and that was then verified to work.

No physical robot data or hardware validation is presented. The Extended system is a desktop cognitive-architecture prototype, not a complete active-inference implementation.

## Files and reproducibility

`manifest_memory_EN.json` records the source file hashes (`cognitive/controller.py`, `cognitive/model.py`, `cognitive/baselines.py`) and which trace file backs each panel. `preview_memory_EN_5s.png`, `_30s.png`, `_65s.png`, `_100s.png` and `_140s.png` are still previews, one per chapter.

To rebuild, from the `Mini_IPHOREOS_EXT1` directory: `python tools/make_ext1_memory_video.py`. Requires Pillow and imageio-ffmpeg (see `requirements.txt`) and a populated `results_ext1/` (run `experiments/run_simulation.py` first if missing).
